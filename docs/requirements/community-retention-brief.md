BAYSE — COMMUNITY & RETENTION DEVELOPMENT BRIEF

For Claude Code | BAYSE Digital Platform

1. BAYSE CLUBS & ONLINE COMMUNITY

Objective

Build a self-sufficient, member-led community within the BAYSE app that connects women both online and in real life.

BAYSE currently operates multiple physical Pilates studios. We want members to build friendships, support one another, organise social activities and feel part of something bigger than attending Pilates classes.

The community must be easy to use, beautifully designed and require minimal administration from BAYSE staff.

The Five Core Features

1. BAYSE Padel Club

A dedicated community where members can organise and join padel games.

Functionality:

* Create a padel match.
* Select date, time and location.
* Specify beginner, intermediate or advanced level.
* Choose doubles or another suitable format.
* Display available player spaces.
* Members can join or leave games.
* Automatically manage waiting lists.
* Create a private group chat for each game.
* Notify nearby interested members about available spaces.
* Allow recurring weekly games.
* Enable members to invite other BAYSE members.

Example:

“Saturday Padel — Altrincham”

10:00 AM | Beginner Friendly

3/4 players confirmed

Join Match

Members arrange their own court bookings and share any costs directly. BAYSE does not need to organise or fund matches.

2. BAYSE Walking Club

A community for members to arrange walks together.

Functionality:

* Create a walking event.
* Choose date, time and public meeting location.
* Add expected distance and difficulty.
* Specify maximum participants if necessary.
* Members can join or leave.
* Group chat for each walk.
* Automatic reminders.
* Recurring weekly walks.
* Discover walks near their studio.

Example:

“Sunday Morning Walk — Hale”

9:30 AM | 5 km | Easy Pace

8 members attending

Join Walk

The app coordinates the event automatically.

No BAYSE employee is required to host.

3. BAYSE Social Club

A space for members to organise casual activities and build friendships.

Activities could include:

* Coffee mornings
* Brunch
* Lunch meetups
* Social evenings
* General member meetups

Functionality:

* Member creates an activity.
* Selects venue, date and time.
* Adds a description.
* Sets optional capacity.
* Other members RSVP.
* Participants can chat.
* Automatic event reminders.
* Members can organise recurring meetups.

BAYSE provides the platform, not the event planning.

4. BAYSE Circles

Smaller online groups designed to encourage real friendships, consistency and accountability.

Members can connect based on:

* Their home BAYSE studio
* Shared fitness interests
* Similar class schedules
* Beginner or advanced experience
* Training programmes
* General interests

Functionality:

* Join a Circle.
* Introduce yourself.
* Group chat and discussion feed.
* Share achievements.
* Encourage other members.
* Arrange Pilates classes together.
* Arrange activities outside the studio.
* Participate in group challenges.

Example:

Altrincham Morning Pilates Girls

A group of women who regularly train in the mornings and want to connect outside class.

Members should be able to choose whether they appear in member discovery and whether others can message them.

5. BAYSE Online Community

A central online community for all eligible members, including those who subscribe digitally and those attending physical studios.

Features:

* Main community feed.
* Member posts.
* Photos and comments.
* Reactions and replies.
* Instructor discussions.
* Training questions.
* Recipe sharing.
* Progress celebrations.
* Monthly community challenges.
* Programme-specific discussions.
* Local studio groups.
* Online-only member groups.

Example community areas:

BAYSE Global

All online and studio members.

BAYSE Altrincham

Local studio members.

Strong & Sculpt

Members following the same programme.

Nutrition & Healthy Habits

Recipe ideas, meal inspiration and supportive healthy-living conversations.

New to BAYSE

A welcoming group for new members.

There must not be a separate BAYSE Wellness Club or a requirement for BAYSE to organise workshops, cooking classes or guest-speaker events.

Community User Experience

The Community screen should display:

Top section:

* Welcome message.
* Member’s home studio.
* Their joined communities.
* Upcoming activities.
* New messages.

Main navigation within Community:

* Community Feed
* My Clubs
* Discover Activities
* My Circles
* Messages

Discover screen:

Members can filter activities by:

* Padel
* Walking
* Social
* Studio location
* Distance
* Date
* Experience level where relevant

Each event card should show:

* Activity name
* Public location
* Date and time
* Host’s chosen display name
* Number of members attending
* Spaces available
* Join button

Self-Sufficient Community Automation

Build the following:

1. Member-created events: Members create and manage activities independently.
2. Automatic matching: Recommend relevant activities based on interests and chosen location.
3. Automatic notifications: Notify interested members of new events and available spaces.
4. Automated event management: Manage RSVPs, capacity, waiting lists and cancellations.
5. Group conversations: Automatically create a discussion group for each activity.
6. Recurring activities: Members can repeat events without creating them from scratch.
7. Community moderation: Automated spam protection, member reporting, blocking and administrator review of flagged content.

Activity participation must always be voluntary.

Use public meeting locations rather than displaying private home addresses. Member privacy and reporting tools are essential.

Key development requirement:

BAYSE staff should not need to manually organise or approve every activity.

The platform should handle routine coordination, while staff or moderators retain the ability to intervene in safety or support issues.

2. BAYSE MEMBER RETENTION SYSTEM

Objective

Develop a member engagement and retention system that helps BAYSE create stronger relationships with its existing studio members.

The intention is to make members feel connected to the brand, their instructors and other members.

Rather than relying solely on class attendance, the app should provide ongoing value and a stronger sense of belonging.

Do not build attendance-based traffic-light triggers or inactivity thresholds in this part of the project.

Focus on proactive retention features.

1. Personalised Member Experience

Every BAYSE member should have a personal dashboard.

Include:

* Their home studio.
* Membership information.
* Favourite instructors.
* Upcoming Pilates classes where booking data is available.
* Personal training goals.
* Workout and attendance history.
* Progress and achievements.
* Recommended on-demand classes.
* Upcoming local community activities.

Members should feel BAYSE understands their preferences.

2. Community-Based Retention

Make it easy for members to develop meaningful connections.

Features:

* Local studio community.
* Member introductions.
* BAYSE Circles.
* Padel Club.
* Walking Club.
* Social Club.
* Group conversations.
* Community challenges.
* Member-created activities.

Members should be able to find others who attend similar classes or have shared interests, without exposing private booking or contact information without permission.

The more connected a member feels, the greater the potential for long-term loyalty.

3. Digital Membership Benefits

Give physical studio members additional value outside the studio.

Potential benefits:

* On-demand Pilates workouts.
* Short home sessions.
* Mobility and recovery classes.
* Personalised training programmes.
* Nutrition recipes and meal planning.
* Progress tracking.
* Online community access.
* Optional live digital classes, according to membership entitlement.

Digital access levels must be configurable.

Do not assume every studio membership includes every premium digital benefit.

This allows members to remain engaged while travelling, working from home or unable to attend a physical class.

4. Member Recognition & Progress

Introduce positive recognition and progress features.

Examples:

* First 10 classes completed.
* 25-class milestone.
* 50-class milestone.
* 100-class milestone.
* One-year BAYSE anniversary.
* Completed training programme.
* First community activity.
* Personal consistency milestones.

Functionality:

* Milestone dashboard.
* Achievement badges.
* Personal congratulations.
* Optional community sharing.
* Digital certificates.
* Progress history.
* Positive coach encouragement.

Avoid making progress overly competitive or creating pressure to attend when someone needs rest.

5. Member Relationships & Ongoing Engagement

Build a system that encourages members to feel recognised and valued.

Include:

Member profile

Members can add interests, preferences and goals.

Instructor connection

Members can follow favourite instructors, see upcoming classes and receive relevant announcements.

Community introductions

New members are offered an introduction to their studio community and relevant Circles.

Class buddies

Members can voluntarily connect and arrange classes together.

Challenges

Optional individual and group challenges encouraging consistency and participation.

Member feedback

Simple opportunities to share their experience, suggestions and concerns.

Member benefits

A configurable section for studio benefits, digital access and future partner offers.

Celebrations

Birthdays or membership anniversaries, where members have provided appropriate information and opted into relevant communications.

3. HOW COMMUNITY AND RETENTION CONNECT

The Community and Retention features must work together.

Example: New Studio Member

A woman joins BAYSE Altrincham.

She downloads the BAYSE app.

She selects Altrincham as her home studio, subject to verified membership.

She completes onboarding and indicates she enjoys padel, Pilates and walking.

The app introduces her to:

* BAYSE Altrincham community
* Local Padel Club
* Walking Club
* A Circle suited to her interests
* Relevant on-demand Pilates content

She can introduce herself, find a padel match, connect with other members and arrange a Pilates session with someone.

All of this takes place without a studio manager manually organising activities.

Example: Existing Member

A member has completed 50 studio classes.

The app recognises the milestone.

She receives a personal congratulations message.

She can share her achievement within her BAYSE Circle if she chooses.

The app also suggests a suitable training programme and activities relevant to her interests.

She continues getting value from BAYSE even when she is not physically in the studio.

4. STUDIO MANAGER REQUIREMENTS

The community and retention features must remain operationally lightweight.

Studio managers should have access to:

* Their local studio community overview
* Important member feedback
* Member milestones
* Relevant engagement information
* Flagged community content
* Issues requiring human assistance

They should not be expected to:

* Organise every padel game
* Arrange walking groups
* Book social venues
* Manually match members
* Manage ordinary event waiting lists
* Send routine event reminders
* Moderate every normal conversation

The target is fewer than 30 minutes of routine community administration per studio per week, excluding exceptional support or moderation work. This must be validated in practice.

5. CENTRAL ADMIN CONTROLS

BAYSE headquarters needs to manage the system centrally.

The admin dashboard should allow authorised users to:

* Create and manage studio communities.
* Configure club categories.
* Manage user permissions.
* View club participation.
* View community engagement.
* Review member feedback.
* Moderate reported content.
* Manage community announcements.
* Configure achievement milestones.
* Manage retention-related member benefits.
* Monitor engagement across studios.
* Compare retention and community participation.

Analytics should include:

* Members registered in the app.
* Members joining communities.
* Members attending social activities.
* Members active in Circles.
* On-demand usage among studio members.
* Studio membership retention.
* Studio churn.
* Member satisfaction feedback.

Participation data should be used to understand community value, not to pressure individual members into social activity.

6. DIRECT INSTRUCTIONS TO CLAUDE

Build BAYSE Clubs and Member Retention as integrated sections of the existing BAYSE digital platform.

Do not create a separate application.

Use the existing member authentication, profiles, permissions, studio locations and overall BAYSE design system.

Prioritise:

1. Local studio communities and member discovery.
2. Padel, walking and social activity creation.
3. Automated RSVPs, waiting lists, notifications and activity messaging.
4. BAYSE Circles and the main community feed.
5. Personalised studio member dashboards, achievements, digital benefits and engagement features.

Provide appropriate privacy, moderation and safety controls.

Keep all features mobile-first and easy to use.

Ensure community activity is primarily member-led, with minimal dependence on BAYSE employees.

Do not implement retention trigger rules or inactivity thresholds as part of this brief.

Success means BAYSE members can independently connect, create activities, form friendships, support each other and receive additional value from their membership without creating significant work for BAYSE staff.

Start by reviewing the existing codebase, mapping these features to the current architecture and creating a staged implementation plan. Then build and test each feature end to end.
